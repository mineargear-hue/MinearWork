Stationary Engineer Exam Prep Guide

Doug Minear · C-10 Contractor · Stationary Engineer · 10 min read

The stationary engineer licensing exam is not a casual test. It covers boiler operation, pressure vessels, refrigeration, HVAC, steam systems, and relevant codes across multiple jurisdictions. If you're preparing without a structured approach, you're going to struggle.

What the Exam Tests

Varies by jurisdiction, but core areas are consistent: boiler theory and operation, steam systems, refrigeration cycles, water treatment, pressure vessel safety, and code compliance. Most states have their own licensing authority — check your jurisdiction's specific scope and format before you start studying, because a California exam and a New York exam are structured differently.

Combustion and Efficiency

Combustion analysis is always on the exam. Know your O2 and CO2 relationships, know what excess air does to efficiency, know CO thresholds. This is also operational knowledge you'll use every day on the job — it's worth understanding deeply, not just memorizing for the test.

Water Treatment

Scale, corrosion, oxygen pitting — water treatment is a significant exam topic and a real operational concern. Know your blowdown procedures, know your chemical treatment basics, know what causes each type of boiler damage. If your exam jurisdiction weights this heavily, spend proportional time on it.

Practical vs. Written Exam

Many jurisdictions require both a written exam and a practical component. The practical is harder to study for because it tests judgment and operational decision-making, not just knowledge. Time in the plant matters. If you're sitting for a practical exam without actual boiler room experience, that's the gap to close first.

After the License

The license is the floor, not the ceiling. CEU requirements keep you current in most jurisdictions. Stay current on refrigerant regulations, emissions standards, and any code updates in your area. The field keeps changing — the license just proves you were competent at one point in time.
